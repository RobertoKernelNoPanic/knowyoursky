KnowYourSky · codename: Orion

Amateur project · free · Beta

Don't just point at the sky.
Actually learn it.

KnowYourSky uses real astronomical data — magnitudes, geometries, and true positions, not a stylized illustration — to train you to recognize stars, constellations, and deep-sky objects. The pretext is a game: a lost alien to guide home, one constellation at a time. The result is that tomorrow night, you recognize them yourself — with the naked eye, no phone in hand.

📡 zero network permissions 🌙 night mode 🧪 in beta

Who's behind it

KnowYourSky is made by amateur astronomers, for anyone who loves the sky — from the youngest kids to those who've been observing it for a lifetime. The goal isn't to keep you on a screen, but to actually teach real sky observation.

Why you can trust it

We're not asking you to take our word for it

Apparent magnitude, coordinates, constellation astrometry: every number you see comes from real astronomical data, not a stylized drawing of the sky. Full source details are in the app's Credits.

Apparent magnitude · mv Real astrometry Network required: none

It's not another sky-pointing app

"Point and discover" apps tell you what you're looking at. Here the goal is the opposite: that one day you won't need any app to know.

Augmented-reality apps

You point your phone, read a label, close it again. The sky goes back to being an unreadable drawing the moment you turn the screen off.

KnowYourSky

You train your eye and memory, constellation after constellation, until the recognition sticks even with the screen off — just by looking up.

For anyone looking up

Whether you're 10 years old or have a €2,000 telescope in the garage, the underlying problem is the same: nobody ever actually taught you to read the sky from memory.

Just starting out

Kids and teens

The sky becomes a game with a clear mission: guide a lost alien home through the stars, one constellation at a time. No lectures, just discovery.

Already watching the sky

Amateur astronomers & experts

You can set up a telescope — but without a planetarium app in hand, can you really recognize every constellation in your hemisphere by eye? It's the most common gap among longtime observers, and the least admitted.
